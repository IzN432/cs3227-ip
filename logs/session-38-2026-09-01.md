# Session 38 — 2026-09-01

## Topic
Reviewing auction time changes, settlement idempotency, and persistence consistency.

## Prompts
- Asked about auction edge cases when the system clock is moved backward.
- Clarified that clock changes may represent the world moving through time; the main concern was item or money
  duplication and consistent state.

## Actions
- Traced auction expiration, bidding eligibility, settlement, polling, and persistence behavior.
- Confirmed that only `ACTIVE` auctions are processed and settlement changes them permanently to `SOLD` or
  `INACTIVE`, preventing repeated polling or later clock rollback from settling the same in-memory auction twice.
- Identified exact-deadline, timezone, daylight-saving, settlement-delay, missing-seller, and clock-sensitive-test
  edge cases.
- Identified the principal duplication risk as cross-file persistence rather than clock movement: users and
  listings are saved separately, so a crash after saving a seller payout but before saving the sold auction can
  cause a second payout after restart.
- Recommended a single atomically replaced marketplace snapshot if crash-consistent transfers become required.

## Outcome
Clock shifting and repeated polling do not themselves duplicate items or money. The remaining consistency risk is
an interrupted multi-file save during bidding or settlement; this was documented as a design limitation and no
code was changed.
